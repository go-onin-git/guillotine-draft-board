Waiver priorities and bidding-room outlook

TJ: An active RB is the first job. McCaffrey is the premium target; Walker and Javonte are strong practical alternatives. Preserve conditional bid ordering so winning one primary RB doesn’t accidentally consume budget on redundant choices. Chase Brown doesn’t solve Week 6 because he is on bye. Lamb or Adams may strengthen the long-term roster, but receiver is no longer the immediate hole.

Matt: Secure a legal active WR2. Adams is desirable; Lamb and DeVonta depend on health. Doubs and the wider pool are useful lower-cost routes. Examine Germie Bernard and Harrison rather than treating the estate as the whole wire. Watch Diggs, but don’t rely on a Monday return without a backup.

Colin: Acquire a tight end. Fannin and Ferguson are obvious estate candidates. Do not spend as if Lamar is the only available QB; Daniels and Love already cover this week. Preserve room for Week 7 if possible.

Max: Add Week 6 QB coverage, with Darnold and Jones among the newly released candidates. RB uncertainty requires a second look, but Gibbs’ bye is already coverable with existing players. The next week removes Allen, making a retained QB especially useful.

Millar: Has the third-largest wallet and fewer immediate holes. Can pursue an RB upgrade without desperation, especially given Montgomery’s injury. McCaffrey or Walker would raise the ceiling, while Javonte offers another route.

Pete: Can compete for the premium estate assets without needing to buy a particular slot to field a full team. McCaffrey, Walker or Lamb could improve a good lineup. Maye and Hurts cover this week’s QB byes.

Jayel: Limited to $0 offers and free agents. Monitor JSN before Thursday and Barkley through the week. Has existing receiving alternatives, so an absent JSN need not become an empty slot.

TJ’s $563 and Pete’s $518 create the strongest auction leverage; Millar’s $393 is meaningful. Max can bid $216; Matt $135; Colin $120; Jayel $0. These are spending ceilings, not predictions that anyone should bid the maximum. No unseen pending bids or competing offers are asserted.

The Monday arithmetic

Shoes had 59.80 before Adams; Matt had 81.50 before Stafford. That was a 21.70 gap, with both players active. Adams needed to outscore Stafford by more than 21.70 to pass Matt. A fixed Sunday target of roughly 36 points for Adams was only an estimate dependent on Stafford’s eventual score, not a guaranteed escape number.

Stafford scored 27.32; Adams scored 19.60. The gap grew to 29.42. To clear Matt’s final total, Adams would have needed more than 49.02. To clear Millar’s final 95.26 instead, he needed more than 35.46. He achieved neither.

Shoes’ best hindsight lineup would have swapped Daniel Jones’ 11.08 for Darnold’s 10.30, Ferguson’s 3.40 for Fannin’s 0.90, and Doubs’ 2.10 for Lamb’s 1.90: 82.88, still 12.38 below Millar. This was not a single bench mistake that changed the elimination.
